# 0.225.0 — 2026-08-07

Mantle is a place people spend hours, so this release lets a brain look like
itself. Four areas of the app can now carry a generated backdrop, drawn from the
same catalogue as the avatars and coloured from whichever theme is active.

## Backgrounds

The menu, the header, the chat and the activity column each pick their own
background, or **Off**. Settings → Appearance.

The artwork is generated rather than a stock image, and it reads its colours
from the live theme, so it repaints when the theme or light/dark changes and can
never end up clashing with the rest of the interface. Sixteen styles are on
offer: waves, stripes, glass, weave, blobs and the rest, plus the three scenes.

Only the menu is decorated out of the box. Turning on all four at once is a
decorated app rather than a decorated panel, so the other three start Off.

**Off is a saved choice, not the absence of one.** That distinction matters: it
is what stops a later change to the defaults from quietly switching an area back
on after you have deliberately turned it off.

Legibility drove every default. The artwork is faint, it fades out toward the
top of the menu and activity columns where the labels are densest, and the chat
gets the lightest touch of the four because it is the surface you actually read.

## The style catalogue is now split by purpose

The styles used to be shelved by how they look (Minimalist, Characters, Scenes),
which described the artwork but not the job it does. Now they are split by what
they are for:

- **Avatars** (34) — the portraits, plus Initials, Initial Face, Glyphs and
  Icons. Those four are minimal, but they encode an identity, which is an
  avatar's whole job and meaningless spread across a sidebar.
- **Backgrounds** (16) — everything else from the old Minimalist shelf, plus all
  three scenes.

## Notes

**The default avatar style has moved from Shapes to Thumbs**, because Shapes is
now a background. If your brain ever chose an avatar style, nothing changes: the
choice is kept and still renders, and the picker still shows it selected even
when it now belongs to the background gallery. Only a brain that never picked
one will see its avatars change, and it changes to a style that is actually a
portrait.

Nothing was migrated in the database, and no style was removed. A style renders
exactly as it always did whichever gallery it now sits in; the split governs
what each picker offers, not what can be drawn.

Backgrounds are a brain-level setting, like the colour theme's stored copy, the
logo and the wordmark. They are part of how this Mantle looks to everyone who
signs into it, rather than a per-browser preference.

The official Mantle logo files also now live in `brand/` at the root of the
repository, in both mono and colour, as SVG and PNG.
